# Workflow Discovery — Client Tickets (Stage 1)

Four tickets from the bank. Your job: decide which ones are actually in scope for today's engagement, and which one you'd push back on — a real FDE doesn't build everything a client asks for, and knowing the difference is the actual skill being graded here, not idea generation.

---

**TICKET A** — Priority: High — Filed by: Contact Center Operations
> Tier-1 phone support reps spend roughly 10 minutes per call manually looking up account balances and transaction history in the legacy system. This is driving up average handle time and hold-queue length. We need faster access to this data during live calls.

---

**TICKET B** — Priority: High — Filed by: Fraud & Security
> SMS fraud-alert delivery is failing for approximately 1 in 5 customers. Investigation shows phone numbers in the legacy system are stored in at least 8 different formats, and our alerting pipeline's number-matching logic can't handle the inconsistency. This is a live security gap, not just a data-quality nuisance.

---

**TICKET C** — Priority: High — Filed by: Risk & Compliance
> Wire transfers above our internal threshold currently require a supervisor to manually review and sign a paper approval form. This takes 2–3 business days, has no searchable audit trail, and has already caused at least one missed regulatory reporting deadline.

---

**TICKET D** — Priority: Medium — Filed by: Retail Banking, Product
> Several customers have asked whether they can open new account types — CDs, personal loans — through the same channel they use for everyday banking. Product would like this explored as a potential extension of scope.

---

## What to do with these

Write down, for each ticket: **in scope for today**, **in scope but later**, or **out of scope** — and one sentence on why. There's a real answer here, not four equally valid opinions: A and B are what Stage 2 builds, C is what Stage 3 governs. One of these four should get a clear "out of scope" with a specific reason, not a vague "maybe later."
